# Food culture of Karnataka (working list and page)

**On the site:** the page `#food` (Explore → Food culture) shows the rows of `src/data/food-culture.js`, each with a badge (*source read*, *lead*, *oral*), in six tabs: Old recipes, Daily food, Festival and ritual, Forest and community, Map and To read next. It reuses the Recreation page's rows, filters and district map. To add or upgrade an entry, edit the data file: set `status: 'read'` only when the cited page was opened, and put the book, page and what it says in `evidence`. `npm test` checks that a *read* entry cites something read and that no entry mentions OCR to readers.

## Vijayanagara-era food (Koli 2019)

Vittal Koli's Hampi doctoral thesis *ವಿಜಯನಗರ ಕಾಲದ ಆಹಾರಪದ್ಧತಿ* (archive.org `kuh.967.kol.141171`, 2019) has chapters on crops, customs, vegetarian and meat dishes, drinks and betel, community and festival food, and cooks and kitchens. Entries so far: **holige and the five bhakshyas** and **mandige** (chapter 4 body, pp. 81, 84–86, 101–102), and four entries taken from the thesis's *own chapter summaries* (pp. 10–14): **drinks as medicine** (ch. 5), **tambula** (ch. 5), **meat as a lower-class food** (ch. 4) and **kitchens, utensils and cooks** (ch. 7). These last four state what the author says about period literature; the underlying sources were not checked. Still to read in the body: crops and water works (ch. 2), meal customs (ch. 3), the savoury and vegetable dish lists (ch. 4), community and festival food (ch. 6). The thesis also quotes the Supashastra, which is still not in the collection.

## Soliga and Tulunadu studies

- **Umesh M. O., *ಸೋಲಿಗರ ಪಾರಂಪರಿಕ ಆಹಾರಪದ್ಧತಿ: ಆಧುನಿಕ ಮುಖಾಮುಖಿ*** (PhD, Hampi, 2015; `kuh.618.uma.049508`; supervisor Gangadhar Daivajna). Entries: **Hosaragi habba**, the new-ragi festival (ch. 7, pp. 187–188) and **bamboo shoots and bamboo rice** with the riddle "rice once in sixty years, a curry once a year" (ch. 5, pp. 135–137). Still to read: the Rotti habba (7.2), Biligiri Ranga fair (7.3), Mari habba (7.4), life-cycle foods (ch. 8), the effect of ration shops and LAMPS (ch. 9), mushrooms, honey and fruit (ch. 5), cooking methods and meat (ch. 4).
- **Giriyappa, *ತುಳುನಾಡಿನ ಸಿಹಿನೀರು ಮೀನು ಬೇಟೆಗಾರಿಕೆ ಜಾನಪದೀಯ ಅಧ್ಯಯನ*** (M.Phil, Hampi, 2018; `kuh.mphil.1387.gir.138162`). One entry, **malejji and snake-fish curry** (chapter 2; page numbers are not reliable in the scan text, so the locator names the chapter and section). The fishing methods, traps and fish in Tulu folk literature are not yet entered.
- Health sayings in these studies (what bamboo shoots, tubers, drinks or fish "cure") are recorded as the community's or author's belief and are not medical advice.

## Riddles and rhymes (sources checked)

- **Ogatugalu mattu kathegalu** (ed. L. R. Hegde, Siddapur; `kuh.ag.1355.014466`): has food riddles, but the scanned text separates each riddle from its answer and garbles the words, so no riddle is entered from it; one pointer entry records it. Read it from page images. Riddles are short and traditional, but the collection is a publisher's work, so quote only a few with page numbers.
- **Children's literature theses** (`kuh.777.sho.134776`, Shoba J., 2017; `kuh.709.bas.132005`, A. Basavesha, 2016): these are analyses of children's literature and language, not collections of rhymes or riddles; they cite poets whose work is in copyright. Nothing entered. If a rhymes section is built, take folk rhymes and lullabies from oral collection (the oral-testimony source type) or from collections with a clear licence.
- **Lambani crafts study** (`kuh.505.rav.049397`) and the remaining food sections of the studies already held are still unread.
